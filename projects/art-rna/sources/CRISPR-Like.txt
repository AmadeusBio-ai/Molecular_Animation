

*Structural reference: the experimentally determined Retron-Eco2 assembly, PDB **9LM3**. This is a useful comparison system for RNA–protein animation—not a structure of the newly described ART system.* [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9LM3)

# Recommended direction: “A hidden repertoire of RNA”

**The strongest film would reveal a pattern in a phage genome, follow that pattern into an ensemble of RNA molecules, and then explore what those RNAs might do with a reverse transcriptase.** I would not frame it as a new CRISPR machine cutting DNA: the authors explicitly state that the system’s function remains unknown. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system)

The linked paper is **“Autonomous AI Agents Discover Reverse Transcriptases with Tandem Repeat Arrays.”** It describes array-associated reverse transcriptases, or **ARTs**: jumbo-phage RTs associated with arrays of approximately 200-nucleotide units and a dedicated partner gene. The abstract reports abundant, apparently discrete array-derived RNAs during infection. That combination gives you a distinctive visual story even without inventing a complete mechanism. [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.ai-agents-discover-reverse-transcriptases)

**Source limitation:** the linked PDF exceeded the browser reader’s file-size limit. I verified the accessible abstract, a reproduced paper figure, the authors’ accompanying report, and the experimental structural studies discussed below; I could not inspect every page or supplementary model in the PDF.

Below is the animation direction I would take, including specific molecular interactions, choreography, structural assets, and Blender implementation.

---

## 1. “The hidden rhythm”: reveal the repeat array

**Best use:** opening discovery sequence, graphical abstract, or the film’s signature shot.

### Visual idea

Begin with a dense but readable landscape of DNA. Most of it is visually undifferentiated. As the camera tracks along one region, small sequence motifs acquire the same ivory highlight. The viewer gradually recognizes a rhythm: repeated elements separated by longer, visually distinct intervals.

Then shift from the molecular view into a clean, flattened locus diagram containing the array, RT gene, and partner gene. This directly expresses the discovery’s defining association rather than showing a generic glowing enzyme. The authors identify the noncoding array and accessory protein association as the important new observation, rather than the first discovery of the underlying RT itself. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system)

### Specific movement

Let the first repeated motif appear almost incidentally. Reveal the next two with enough delay that the audience notices the recurrence before the whole array is highlighted.

The DNA should continue its gentle movement while the **annotation** becomes organized. Avoid making the molecule physically rearrange itself into a perfect barcode. A camera match-cut from a curved DNA segment to a flat genomic diagram is cleaner than an apparently biological straightening event.

For the sequence-level close-up, highlight the matching letters while leaving intervening sequence unhighlighted. The reproduced paper figure actually shows the discovery emerging from direct inspection of DNA sequence, making this a paper-specific visual rather than a generic “AI scanning” effect. [alphaXiv](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/paper-figures/01a0cfad-55c8-76d5-95c3-f930d33bae55/01a0cfad-ad04-71cf-811c-1d7583b77bc5.jpg)

### Blender treatment

Create a nucleotide-level attribute system with custom fields such as:

`base_index`, `base_type`, `repeat_id`, `is_repeat`, and `locus_region`.

Use those attributes to drive materials and annotation visibility. Keep the molecular geometry unchanged when a repeat is highlighted.

For layout development, a simplified array is fine, but label it **schematic**. For the final film, use the selected locus’s actual annotated boundaries rather than treating every approximately 200-nucleotide unit as one identical repeat.

### Why it could look beautiful

A restrained palette will make the discovery legible: warm amber DNA, quiet neutral sequence, and ivory repeat highlights. The visual payoff is the emergence of order from complexity—not an explosion of particles.

**Matching still illustration:** a shallow arc of DNA with repeated motifs aligned beneath it as a genomic strip, with the RT and partner-gene annotations occupying the negative space.

## 2. “Inside a jumbo phage”: establish the correct biological setting

**Best use:** a brief opening shot, not a lengthy infection sequence.

For an SA1-specific exterior, there is usable morphological evidence: the original characterization describes a polyhedral head approximately **100 nm across**, a tail approximately **220 nm long**, a baseplate, and tail fibers. The source experiments used **S. lentus JTB1-3** to isolate and propagate the phage. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9096886/)

### Visual idea

Place a small number of phages near a curved, staphylococcal cell surface. Frame one phage obliquely so the head-to-tail proportions read immediately.

The camera moves past the capsid and transitions into the genome sequence—not necessarily through a literal cutaway of DNA injection.

### Specific movement

Use slow, irregular translation and rotational drift for the free phage. Give the tail fibers restrained local movement rather than making them walk, grasp, or actively steer.

For an attachment moment, let the body’s free rotation become constrained near the surface. Stop before depicting a species-specific receptor interaction or detailed tail-contraction sequence unless that mechanism has been separately verified.

### Blender treatment

Model the exterior from the published morphology at the level it supports. Do not substitute a highly detailed T4 model and present it as SA1.

A gently displaced cell surface, a few out-of-focus neighboring particles, and broad grazing illumination should be enough. The composition does not need a dense “molecular soup.”

**Production choice:** spend perhaps eight to ten seconds here. The repeat-array and RNA scenes are more distinctive to this paper than a generic phage infection spectacle.

## 3. “The RNA repertoire”: turn genomic repetition into molecular diversity

**Best use:** the central ART-specific sequence.

The paper’s abstract reports that ART arrays are highly expressed and appear as discrete units during infection. It suggests a repertoire of distinct RNAs, but that observation does not establish their exact three-dimensional folds or how each RNA interacts with the RT. [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.ai-agents-discover-reverse-transcriptases)

### Visual idea

Return to the highlighted array. Its individual units become associated with separate RNA strands arranged at different depths.

Initially, keep each RNA visually connected to its genomic origin through a thin editorial guide line. Remove those guide lines as the camera enters the RNA ensemble.

The result should feel like a collection of related but nonidentical molecules—not identical beads on a production line.

### Specific movement

I would separate this sequence into two visual statements:

**Expression:** establish that RNA products arise from the array.

**Possible conformation:** show selected RNA strands adopting illustrative or predicted conformations.

Use an edit between these statements. Do not automatically show one long transcript being cut into pieces by a nuclease: that would assert a processing mechanism that has not been verified in the accessible ART materials.

For the folding shot, allow different regions to move at different rates. A short segment can settle into a local stem while another remains mobile. Avoid the common animation shortcut in which an entire RNA collapses into its final shape simultaneously.

### Blender treatment

Build each RNA as a fixed-length backbone with separately controlled bases. Preserve nucleotide order throughout the shot.

For an illustrative fold, use paired control points or explicit base-pair constraints rather than unrestricted noise on a curve. When using a computational prediction, identify it on screen as **predicted structure** and retain alternative conformations where uncertainty matters.

A useful visual device is to show one solid candidate conformation and two faint alternatives. The alternatives should be presented as uncertainty, not as measured intermediate states.

### Why it could look beautiful

Use teal RNA against the amber genomic region. Give the RNAs different silhouettes, but keep their material language consistent.

A slow lateral camera move through three depth layers can produce a sophisticated shot without requiring excessive deformation. Let one foreground RNA provide a strong compositional curve while the others remain recognizable in the background.

**Matching still illustration:** several RNA conformations beneath the annotated array, with genomic provenance clearly shown and “illustrative/predicted folds” stated in the legend.

## 4. “Copying without consuming”: an atomic reverse-transcription sequence

**Best use:** the principal mechanistic close-up, explicitly labeled as an established RT comparison.

A particularly useful starting asset is **PDB 6AR1**, a group II intron reverse transcriptase from *Geobacillus stearothermophilus*. The deposited complex contains an RNA template, DNA primer, incoming **dATP**, and magnesium. It is an experimental structure, but it is **not ART**. [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/6ar1)

### Visual idea

Start with a surface representation of the protein embracing the RNA–DNA duplex. Slowly remove a small section of the surface—not the whole protein—to reveal the active site.

The RNA remains teal. The DNA primer is amber. The incoming nucleotide uses the DNA palette, with its phosphate groups distinguished for the chemistry close-up.

The narrative should be:

> The RNA provides information. A separate DNA strand is extended.

Do not morph RNA into DNA.

### Specific molecular choreography

Begin with the primer already positioned in the enzyme. This avoids implying a particular ART initiation mechanism.

An incoming complementary nucleotide samples orientations near the active site, then reaches the structurally supported bound pose. Hold this pose long enough for the template base, incoming base, and primer end to be understood.

For a chemically explicit explanatory reconstruction, the primer’s **3′ oxygen** forms a bond to the incoming nucleotide’s **α-phosphate**, while the β- and γ-phosphates depart together as pyrophosphate. The DNA strand is extended at its 3′ end. Treat the interpolated reaction and subsequent translocation as an explanatory animation, not a trajectory measured by the static structure. The structural study provides the template–primer and incoming-nucleotide arrangement for this comparison. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1097276517307992?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

After incorporation, shift the template–primer register by one nucleotide. Keep this modest: the protein should not close like a mechanical claw or perform a large piston stroke without supporting conformational data.

### An important asset correction

The structural experiment used a **dideoxynucleotide at the primer’s 3′ end** to prevent extension and trap the complex. Therefore, the deposited primer should not simply be animated undergoing normal incorporation unchanged. For a catalytic reconstruction, make a documented, chemically reviewed derivative model with a normal reactive primer terminus. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1097276517307992?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Also audit the deposited ligands. The presence of a ligand in a coordinate file does not automatically make it part of the physiological reaction; 6AR1 includes sulfate as well as dATP and magnesium. [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/6ar1)

### Blender treatment

Use three separate control systems:

- A rigid or restrained protein scaffold.
- A template–primer register control.
- A nucleotide/reaction control with explicit pre- and post-reaction geometry.

Do not implement bond formation by stretching a cylinder indefinitely between moving atoms. Use chemically consistent endpoint models and a deliberately simplified transition.

For the hero frame, keep most of the protein as a softly lit surface, display the duplex as a backbone-and-base representation, and show only the relevant active-site atoms as sticks.

**On-screen label:** “Established reverse-transcriptase chemistry — comparison system.”

## 5. “RNA as architecture”: a structurally grounded molecular-origami sequence

**Best use:** a companion animation or a short comparison explaining why an RNA-associated RT system is intriguing.

For this, **Retron-Eco2, PDB 9LM3**, is especially useful. Its experimentally determined assembly contains three RT–Toprim proteins and three RNA–DNA components arranged in a triangular complex. RNA from one component interacts with a neighboring protein. The associated density map is **EMD-63214**. [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9LM3)

### Visual idea

Begin with the complete triangular assembly, then selectively hide the proteins to reveal the RNA paths.

Rather than making the assembly spin continuously, move the camera through one corner of the triangle. Reveal how one RNA spans neighboring subunits, then return to the complete structure.

This can communicate “RNA is part of the architecture” far more effectively than a free-floating RNA strand approaching a generic protein blob.

### Specific interactions worth illustrating

These contacts are reported for **Eco2**, not ART. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41421-025-00823-y)

| Structural feature | Suggested animation |
|---|---|
| **rG15–dT1:** a branched **2′,5′ phosphodiester linkage** connects RNA and DNA. | Follow the RNA backbone, pause at the branch, and reveal the DNA connection with a small change in viewing angle. |
| **rG15–R184:** the RNA guanosine forms hydrogen-bonding contacts with an arginine. | Fade in the relevant atoms and short dashed contact lines. Keep the interaction spatially subtle. |
| **rU51–F46:** a flipped-out RNA base stacks against an aromatic phenylalanine side chain. | Reveal the nearly parallel aromatic surfaces in an edge-on view. Use lighting and camera position, rather than glowing “magnetic” attraction, to explain the contact. |

These are **structural observations, not observed movies of binding**. I would animate their revelation more confidently than their formation. In particular, an outward-facing base in a structure does not establish when or how it flipped outward.

### Blender treatment

Import both the atomic model and density. A brief transition from a softly rendered density envelope to the fitted model can establish the evidence behind the geometry.

Use an exploded view only as a labeled explanatory diagram. Do not present the reverse of an exploded view as a measured self-assembly pathway.

**Matching still illustration:** a triangular RNA–protein assembly with one corner enlarged into an atomic contact inset.

## 6. “The unresolved partner”: make uncertainty visually interesting

**Best use:** the closing ART-specific sequence.

The associated accessory protein is one of the system’s defining features, but the authors describe its function as unknown. Genomic association should therefore not be translated automatically into a particular binding interface or fixed molecular complex. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system)

### Visual idea

Show the array, RT gene, and partner gene in the genomic view. Then introduce their potential molecular counterparts in an explicitly hypothetical composition.

Keep the RT, RNA, and partner separated at first. Instead of choosing a single dramatic docking pose, display a few alternative relationship diagrams:

**Does an array RNA serve as a template? Does the partner interact with the RT? Does it interact with RNA?**

These should be questions, not three asserted mechanisms.

### Specific movement

Use subtle independent motion in each proposed arrangement. When switching hypotheses, cross-fade between compositions rather than having the same molecules fly through a sequence that looks like a validated reaction pathway.

For an illustrative encounter, show imperfect orientation and an unsuccessful contact rather than every collision producing immediate binding. However, do not use that encounter to imply a particular ART binding surface.

### Blender treatment

Until an author-provided or otherwise traceable ART model is available, use a clearly schematic representation—not an unrelated PDB structure relabeled as ART.

For predicted models, retain model provenance and confidence information in the project. A polished molecular surface should not erase the difference between a prediction and an experimentally determined structure.

### Ending image

Return to the genomic array. Place the RNA repertoire beneath it and the two protein components nearby, linked by restrained question marks or dashed diagrammatic connections.

A strong closing line would be:

> **The components are emerging. Their molecular relationship remains to be resolved.**

## 7. Optional standalone loop: an RNA-scaffolded retron filament

For an additional portfolio-quality sequence, **Retron-Eco1** provides a different architectural motif: a filament rather than a triangle. **PDB 8QBM** contains a filament segment with ADP-ribosylated effector; its associated map is **EMD-18315**. The original study describes an RNA–DNA scaffold participating in an assembly that restrains the effector. Again, this is a comparison system, not an ART mechanism. [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/8QBM)

I would design a 15–20-second loop that moves along the filament, reveals a longitudinal cutaway, and isolates one repeating segment. Use the deposited assembly relationships; do not simply repeat a monomer with an arbitrary screw transform.

There is also a directly relevant professional animation reference: the University of Copenhagen’s Montoya Group hosts a **Retron-Eco1 animation credited to Janet Iwasa**, accompanying the 2024 study. That is a much more relevant reference to examine than a generic molecular-animation showreel. [CPR](https://www.cpr.ku.dk/research/protein-structure-function-program/montoya/videos/)

# Making the molecular motion convincing

## Separate camera motion from molecular motion

My recommendation is to obtain most of the cinematic energy from camera movement, selective visibility, and representation changes.

A stable structure can be visually compelling when the camera reveals a hidden interface. It does not need to deform continuously.

For free molecules, use independent translational and rotational variation. Avoid synchronized sine-wave motion across an entire scene, which makes molecules look like coordinated swimmers.

## Use restrained, hierarchical deformation

For an artistic motion pass, I would separate the controls into:

**Whole-complex motion:** translation and rotation.

**Domain motion:** restrained movement around selected hinges.

**Local motion:** limited flexibility in exposed loops and nucleic-acid segments.

Treat this as illustrative motion unless it comes from a justified simulation or experimentally supported conformational series. Do not label a Geometry Nodes animation “molecular dynamics” merely because it contains random movement.

Blender’s Simulation Zones can carry state between frames and support baked procedural simulations, but they do not by themselves supply a validated molecular force field. [Blender Documentation](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/modeling/geometry_nodes/simulation/simulation_zone.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Preserve topology before adding beauty

The most important review questions are not whether the surface looks realistic. They are whether the chains remain connected, the correct ends are being extended, the RNA and DNA retain their identities, and the molecules avoid implausible intersections.

For RNA animation, I would prioritize fixed nucleotide count, stable base identity, explicit strand direction, and controlled base pairing. For atomic reactions, I would use separately reviewed endpoint structures.

## Distinguish editorial time from biological time

A six-second nucleotide-incorporation shot should be understood as an explanatory expansion, not a claim about six seconds of real chemistry.

Likewise, an RNA-expression montage should not imply that all transcripts appear simultaneously. Put quantitative clocks on screen only when the underlying experiment supports them.

# Blender production approach

## Structural assets and provenance

Use **Molecular Nodes** as the molecular-data import and representation layer. Its documentation supports PDB/mmCIF import, molecular-dynamics trajectories, and electron-microscopy maps, making it well suited to switching between atomic models, surfaces, and experimental envelopes. [Brady Johnston](https://bradyajohnston.github.io/MolecularNodes/)

| Asset | Appropriate use | Important restriction |
|---|---|---|
| **6AR1** | RT/template–primer active-site comparison. | Not ART; review the blocked primer and ligand content before animating catalysis. |
| **9LM3 / EMD-63214** | Eco2 RNA-mediated architecture and atomic contacts. | Not evidence of ART stoichiometry or partner binding. |
| **8QBM / EMD-18315** | Eco1 filament and scaffold comparison. | Do not transfer its filament or effector mechanism to ART. |
| **ART sequence, locus annotations, and any traceable predictions** | Paper-specific array and hypothetical protein scenes. | Exact files and model provenance still need to be checked against the full paper and supplements. |

The experimental entries and map associations above are documented in their structural records or original study. [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/6ar1)

For each asset, keep a small manifest recording the source, biological assembly, chain identifiers, missing regions, altered residues or termini, and whether its movement is experimental, simulated, or illustrative.

One practical trap is confusing a crystallographic asymmetric unit with a biological assembly. Another is confusing author chain IDs with the identifiers used by the imported file. Resolve both before rigging.

## Suggested scene organization

I would separate the Blender project into collections for:

`SOURCE_COORDINATES`, `DISPLAY_GEOMETRY`, `MOTION_RIGS`, `ANNOTATIONS`, and `LIGHTING`.

Keep the imported coordinates untouched. Build representation changes and motion on derived objects or controlled geometry.

This makes scientific revisions much easier: changing a label, chain selection, or assembly should not require rebuilding the whole shot.

## Representation changes

Use three deliberate levels of detail:

**Overview:** molecular surfaces and simplified nucleic-acid backbones.

**Interface:** protein cartoon or selectively removed surface, with identifiable RNA bases.

**Chemistry:** only the relevant atoms, ligands, and contact annotations.

Avoid showing every atom throughout the film. It reduces readability and makes the important interactions less distinctive.

## Art direction

My preferred palette would be **amber DNA, teal RNA, muted violet RT, coral partner, and ivory annotations**. Keep identity consistent between the genomic diagram and the molecular shots.

For materials, I would favor softly rough surfaces over polished plastic or metallic proteins. Use slightly different roughness and value for RNA and DNA so they remain distinguishable even when the colors become subdued.

Light the scene with a broad key, a restrained edge light, and enough fill to read cavities. Keep the active site brighter through composition and local lighting—not through excessive emission.

For camera work, start with roughly **70–100 mm lenses** for molecular portraits, modest depth of field, and slow lateral or orbital moves. These are art-direction starting points, not scientific parameters.

AgX is worth testing as the display transform, but watch highlight exposure: its treatment of highly exposed colors can reduce the identity conveyed by saturated materials. [Blender Documentation](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/color_management/displays_views.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Rendering and review

My production preference would be to render image sequences with separate annotation control and object masks, rather than baking every label into the beauty pass.

Before final rendering, review an unlit or simple-shaded version. Ask whether the scientific story still reads without bloom, depth of field, or dramatic lighting. That review often catches topology and labeling problems that a beautiful render conceals.

# A practical two-minute main film

I would keep Eco2 and Eco1 as optional companion animations so the main film remains centered on ART.

| Time | Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **0–10 s** | SA1-inspired phage and host context. | Establish where the genomic system is found. |
| **10–32 s** | DNA sequence becomes a repeat-array diagram. | Reveal the defining pattern. |
| **32–52 s** | Array-associated RNA repertoire. | Show the experimental observation without inventing processing or folding mechanisms. |
| **52–80 s** | Clearly labeled 6AR1-based RT comparison. | Explain copying RNA information into a separate DNA strand. |
| **80–108 s** | Return to ART: RNA, RT, and partner hypotheses. | Make the unresolved molecular relationships explicit. |
| **108–120 s** | Clean array/RNA/protein composition. | End on the discovery and the next mechanistic question. |

**I would build the repeat-array → RNA-repertoire transition first.** It is the most distinctive visual contribution of this paper. Then develop the RT active-site comparison as the detailed molecular centerpiece, and reserve the retron architectures for separate sequences where their specific contacts can be explored without being mistaken for ART biology.
